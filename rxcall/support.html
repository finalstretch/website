Support

Rxcall

Rxcall keeps your medication list on your phone and checks it against public FDA recall data, so the one recall that matters to you actually reaches you.

Get help

Email us at hello@finalstretch.org with as much detail as you can: what you did, what you expected, and what happened instead. Screenshots help.

Rxcall is built by two of us, so replies can take a few days, but we read everything.

Common questions

Is my medication list private?
Yes. It never leaves your phone. Recall matching happens on your device against the FDA’s public recall feed; the app has no accounts and no servers of ours. See the privacy policy.

Does Rxcall cost anything?
No. No price, no in-app purchases, no ads. Staying free is one of the commitments we started the project with.

Rxcall says something I take was recalled. Should I stop taking it?
Don’t decide alone, and don’t stop a medication on an app’s word. Many recalls affect only specific lots or manufacturers, and stopping some medications abruptly is dangerous. Rxcall shows you what the recall actually says. Bring it to your pharmacist or doctor; they can check your exact bottle.

Why didn’t Rxcall alert me about a recall I heard of?
Rxcall matches against the FDA’s published enforcement data, which can lag news reports, and matching depends on how the recall names the product. If you think the app missed something it should have caught, email us the recall and the single entry from your list (never send your full list). Matching accuracy is the whole point of this app, so we treat misses as serious bugs.

Is Rxcall medical advice?
No. Rxcall surfaces public recall information relevant to you. Decisions about your medications belong with you, your pharmacist, and your doctor.

Open source

Rxcall’s code will be published under the Apache 2.0 license at github.com/finalstretch once the app lands on the App Store. From then on, bug reports and feature requests are welcome there as GitHub issues; until then, email works just as well.

The Final Stretch Project is a free, open-source project.
